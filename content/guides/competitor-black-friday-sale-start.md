Black Friday 2026 is on **Friday, 27 November**. Most of your competitors won't wait for it. Many start with early access for their email list, then open the sale to everyone days before the weekend. If you find out from a customer, you've already lost the first few days.

Here's how to see a competitor's sale the day it starts. The first four ways are free and manual; the last one is automatic.

## 1. Check what they did last year

Last year's timing is the best guide to this year's. Use the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org) to look at a competitor's homepage on each day of November 2025:

1. Go to web.archive.org and enter their homepage, for example `dewlane.com`.
2. Open the 2025 calendar and click the days in mid to late November.
3. Note the first day a sale banner appears, and what it says (the discount, any code, "early access").

Popular stores are saved most days; smaller ones may only be saved a few times a month, so treat the date as approximate.

## 2. Join their email list and SMS

Early access almost always goes to subscribers first. Sign up to each competitor's list with an address you only use for this, and turn on their texts if they offer them. The day "VIP early access" lands in that inbox is the day their sale really starts.

## 3. Watch their ads

Brands usually start running sale ads the day the sale goes live. The [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) is public: search a competitor's brand name and you'll see the Facebook and Instagram ads they're running now, each with the date it started running. A new batch of ads mentioning a discount is a strong sign their sale has begun.

## 4. Look at their sale page and product prices

On the site itself, three things change when a sale starts:

- **A sale collection appears or fills up.** Many Shopify stores have a page like `/collections/sale`; some add a dedicated `/collections/black-friday`. Bookmark it and check it each morning in November.
- **Products show a crossed-out price.** On Shopify, that's the "compare-at" price: the original price shown next to the sale price. When it appears across most of the catalog at once, the sale is sitewide.
- **The homepage banner changes.** Look for a discount code or "ends Monday" wording.

Checking one or two competitors by hand is manageable. Checking five every morning for a month is where it falls apart.

## What to do once you know

Knowing the date is only useful if you act on it. A few options, depending on your margins:

- **Match the timing, not the discount.** Open your own early access the same day, for your list only, without going deeper than you planned.
- **Lead with a different offer.** If they go 25% off sitewide, a gift with purchase or free shipping threshold can compete without starting a price war.
- **Hold your price on your hero product.** If it's not on sale at theirs, it doesn't need to be at yours.

## FAQ

**When do most brands start their Black Friday sales?**
It varies by brand and year, which is why checking each competitor's own history (step 1) is more useful than an industry average.

**Is it OK to watch a competitor's prices?**
Checking competitors' public websites is everyday practice in retail: prices, products and sale banners are shown to every visitor. Trailwatch only reads public pages, never logs in, and respects each site's robots.txt.

**Does this only work for Shopify stores?**
Steps 1–4 work for any store. Trailwatch's automatic sale alerts need the store's public product catalog, which most Shopify stores have; for other stores it watches their homepage and sale page instead.
